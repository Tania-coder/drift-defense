Design doctrine

A binary, held honestly.

Leibniz published binary arithmetic in 1703 and, in the same paper, mapped it onto the I Ching's broken and unbroken lines. That is the whole job here: take a continuous signal and reduce it to one honest verdict. The six-line figure on each leg above is not decoration — each line is a real check, drawn unbroken for yes, broken for no, dotted where the honest answer is not yet knowable.

This page says yes to

  • Real numbers, or an explicit unknown. Silence renders as silence.
  • Ornament that is also instrument — the hexagram is the readout.
  • One accent colour, spent only on meaning: state, never mood.
  • Serif for the argument, monospace for the measurement. The two registers stay visibly distinct.
  • Negative space as structure, so the eye is led rather than crowded.
  • Motion only where it carries information.
  • Zero third-party fonts, CDNs or trackers beyond cookieless counts.
  • Readable by machines: weather.json, llms.txt, JSON-LD.
  • Keyboard focus, reduced-motion and contrast honoured by default.

And no to

  • A fake “AI is thinking…” stream. Nothing here pretends to be a live mind.
  • An invented probability. A number with no calibration behind it is a lie in a lab coat.
  • Green by default. An unmonitored leg is amber, because that is the truth.
  • Stock gradient blobs, glassmorphism, and the same hero every other tool ships.
  • Carousels, modals, cookie walls, chat bubbles, countdown timers.
  • Webfonts that leak the reader to a third party on a privacy product.
  • Frameworks and build steps for a page that is one document.
  • Autoplaying video, parallax, and scroll-jacking.
  • Claims the repository cannot back.

The same doctrine governs the engine: status precedence is DRIFTING > STALE > STABLE, so a leg that has stopped reporting can never publish as healthy. A monitor that shows green because it cannot hear anything is the exact failure this project exists to prevent.

FAQ

Straight answers.

How is this different from Datadog / LangSmith / eval tools?

App-level observability tells you your outputs got worse. It can't tell you whether the model itself changed — and it's often channel-conflicted (it sells to the providers it would have to indict). SEISMOGRAPH watches the provider side, neutrally, across time.

What do I actually get?

The free scan: your top-3 drift exposures within ~48h. The Baseline: live canary probes, tuned thresholds, a written report, and an alerting plan — a reviewable deliverable in about a week, not an open-ended retainer.

Is my data safe?

Raw prompts and outputs never leave your perimeter — only DP-noised aggregate features. Probes run in your VPC. You own every artifact. Full technical detail is in the open-source repo.

Why should I trust the detection?

Because it's falsifiable. The 38-day Claude Sonnet 4 call was made on a seeded, reproducible backtest you can re-run. The methodology is hash-committed up front.

What is the board at the top actually showing?

The public gateway's /v1/weather endpoint, fetched on a six-hour schedule by a scheduled job and committed next to this page, so your browser reads it instantly and no request of yours ever touches the gateway. The baseline, z-score and CUSUM statistic are recomputed from the observations accumulated since that job started — which is why some lines read unknown until enough points exist to answer honestly.

What isn't ready yet?

Honest answer: the public network effect (cross-org quorum) compounds with adoption and is early. Today the value is the in-VPC early-warning layer and the neutral monitoring — which stand on their own. I'd rather tell you that than oversell it.
